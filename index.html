Thesis in progress University of Turku CSC supercomputing

Prognostic modelling of early brain development using radiomic features from the Calgary Preschool MRI Dataset

Children in the Calgary cohort were scanned repeatedly between roughly ages two and eight and assessed with NEPSY II and Woodcock Johnson IV language tasks. I built an end to end pipeline on CSC's HPC cluster to ask a narrow question: once you know a child's age and sex, does their brain scan add anything to predicting phonological processing and speeded naming?

143children in the cohort
2,023T1 scans segmented
14,469radiomic features per scan
460diffusion scans analysed
  1. 01 SegmentSynthSeg on every T1 image, with visual QC of the label maps
  2. 02 ExtractPyRadiomics shape, intensity and texture features per brain region
  3. 03 HarmoniseComBat across five scanner software versions, protecting age
  4. 04 ModelElastic Net, Random Forest and TabPFN in stratified cross validation
  5. 05 ExtendDTI with DIPY: FA and MD per region from the diffusion scans
Finding

Age and sex explain nearly all of the predictable variance. Single scan radiomics, longitudinal change, socioeconomic factors and diffusion measures added no reliable value on top.

Finding

Scanner software version was the largest single source of variance in the radiomic features, ahead of age, which is why harmonisation and stratification mattered.

Finding

TabPFN matched or beat the classical models in every comparison, but its edge did not survive correction for multiple testing.
